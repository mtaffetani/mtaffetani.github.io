      <h3>Morphing in Slender Structures</h3>
      <p>We explore the hidden mechanics that make slender and thin-walled structures bend, buckle, wrinkle, and snap. From arches and curved strips to shells, we are moved by the question <i>what determines how and when a structure changes shape, forms patterns, or jumps to a new stable state?</i> Using mathematical modeling, stability analysis, and numerical simulations, I uncover how geometry, curvature, internal stresses, and even tiny imperfections govern these dramatic behaviors. The works in this project section reveal not just when structures fail, but how they <b> organize into ridges, wrinkles or bistable configurations </b>, providing principles that guide the design of responsive, bio-inspired, and functional materials capable of harnessing instabilities and patterns for novel mechanical functionality.</p>

      <h3>Instabilities in Elasticity</h3>
      <p>We explore how soft elastic materials spontaneously buckle, bead, wrinkle, and finger under external forces. Unlike slender structures, where geometry often dominates and instabilities are largely dictated by shape and boundary constraints, bulk systems involve complex interactions between elasticity, surface forces, and volume effects, making their behavior richer and more sensitive to imperfections. What determines the wavelength of a bead, the pattern of a wrinkle, or the onset of fingering in a bulk material? Our research shows that these phenomena emerge from a delicate balance of elastic energy, geometry, and applied forces, and that even tiny inhomogeneities can dramatically influence the patterns.

By combining mathematical modeling, nonlinear stability analysis, and simulations, we uncover the principles that govern these instabilities, providing insight into how to control and exploit them. This understanding opens the door to designing responsive, bio-inspired, and functional materials where instabilities are not just a failure mode but a tool for innovation.</p>
